Building a Custom Template

Back to Knowledge Base

Every situation asks for its own analysis. With a template you customise the report completely: you pick the measurements that matter to you, set the limits you work with, and go as deep as the situation needs. This article covers the template builder step by step, from choosing the input source to publishing.

1 What a Template Is

A template is a report you design yourself. You decide four things:

PartWhat you decide
Input sourcesWhich data the report uses, such as an Xsens motion capture recording
Input pageWhat the evaluator fills in before processing: the questions, and the fields asked for every task
Result pagesWhat the report shows and how: which measurements, with which zones, in which blocks and in which order
Name & tileHow the template appears on the New Project page: name, category, description and image

Once published, the template appears as a tile on the New Project page, in the category you chose, next to the built-in assessment methods. Reports made with it are saved, exported and filtered like any other report.

A template is not a validated method

Motionprint Ergo measures the variables; the zones and limits are yours. A template is as sound as the thresholds you set. Use limits from a standard, a guideline or your own company policy, and name the source in the template's description.

2 Opening the Builder

Go to Templates in the sidebar. The page shows every template as a tile with its status: Draft, or Published · Vn with its version number. The category chips above the tiles filter the list.

  • Click New template to start from scratch.
  • Click a template and choose Edit to change it, Duplicate to start from a copy, or Delete to remove it.
  • Click Edit categories to rename categories or change the colour of their tiles.

The builder has four steps, shown at the top of the screen: Sources, Input page, Result pages and Save. You can move between them at any time; your work is saved as a draft as you go.

3 Sources

Choose the input sources the template uses. Today that is Xsens motion capture; reference video is coming soon, and more input sources will be added over time.

Below the tiles, Available variables lists what the selected sources can measure. The chips filter the list by how well a variable is measured: Reliable or Indicative (an estimate). You do not pick variables here; you pick them later, on the result pages, where you need them.

With Xsens selected, the variables come in five groups:

GroupVariables available with Xsens
MovementSpeed, acceleration, angular velocity and angular acceleration of a body part; foot on the floor; walking path
PositionPosition of a body point, for example the hand relative to the shoulder or the floor
Arm anglesUpper arm flexion/extension and abduction, shoulder elevation, elbow flexion, forearm rotation, wrist flexion/extension and deviation
Leg anglesHip flexion/extension, knee flexion, ankle flexion
Neck & trunk anglesPelvis tilt, head inclination, neck flexion, rotation and lateral bending, trunk inclination, back bending, trunk twist, pelvis rotation

Angles are in degrees, positions and distances in metres, speeds in m/s. Most variables can be measured for the left and the right side.

4 Input Page

The input page is what the evaluator fills in before processing. It always holds the general information (workplace, evaluator, operator, date, age, gender) and the recording. You add your own questions in rows around it.

Laying out the page

A page is built from rows, and every row is split into containers that hold one block each.

  • Add a row — click the + tile below the last row and choose its layout: one full-width container, two halves, three thirds, a third and two thirds, or two thirds and a third.
  • Insert a row — click the gap between two rows to add a row at that place.
  • Move a row — hover a row and use the up and down arrows beside it. The general information moves the same way.
  • Delete a row — hover a row and click its delete icon; you are asked to confirm.

Adding and moving blocks

Open Basic blocks in the bar at the top and drag a block into an empty container, marked Drop a block here. To move a block, drag it to an empty container in the same or another row.

BlockUse it for
Button choiceA choice from a few options, shown as buttons
DropdownA choice from a longer list
Number fieldA typed number, with unit and range
Text fieldFree text
Yes/NoA single yes/no question
ChecklistSeveral options that can be ticked, with an optional maximum
ScaleA value on a slider, with minimum, maximum and step
TimeA duration
Multiple linesSeveral fields on lines below each other in one block, also next to each other
3D viewThe recording, with the task window (see below)
When a block does not fit

Some blocks need room. A block with two fields next to each other needs at least half a row, and the 3D view with its tasks needs the full width. While you drag, a container that is too narrow turns red and says Too narrow for this block; dropping it there tells you what the block needs.

Block settings

Click a block to select it. Its Block settings open in the panel beside the page: title, info text, options with their score, whether it is Required, an optional image, and Delete block. The panel can be hidden and shown again with its button. Changes show on the page straight away, and you can try the block out there.

Task fields

Tasks are parts of the recording marked on the timeline. Start frame, end frame and description are always there. In the settings of the 3D view you can add fields to the task window, such as the weight of the load or a yes/no for coupling. These are asked for every task. Here you also set whether at least one task is required.

Saved blocks

Click Save to block library on a block you will use again. It then appears under Saved blocks, in a group of your choice, ready to drag into other templates.

5 Result Pages

The result pages are the tabs of the report that show the data. A new template has two, as the other reports do: Score overview and In-depth analysis. Rename them with the pencil beside the tab. The Score overview is always in the report; the In-depth analysis can be switched on or off.

You build a result page the same way as the input page: rows, containers and blocks. With tasks, a task selector heads every result page, so the reader can switch between tasks.

Result blocks

BlockShows
General infoWorkplace, evaluator, operator, date and the number of tasks; optionally the station time
Score cardA meter: a half circle in the colours of the zones, with a needle at the value. It can also show the time or the percentage of time in one zone
Graph over timeThe measurement over the recording, with its zones as coloured bands and the tasks marked
Time per zoneHow much of the time is spent in each zone, as bars or a pie
Walking pathThe route walked, seen from above, with its distance
Input answerThe answer given to a field on the input page, coloured by its zone
TextA text of your own, such as an explanation of the results
3D viewThe recording, with the blocks on the page following it as it plays

General info, Graph over time and Time per zone need at least half a row.

Choosing what a block measures

Select a Score card, Graph over time or Time per zone and choose its variable in the settings. Depending on the variable you then choose how it is measured, for example the direction of a position or whether a speed is horizontal only, and the Side: right, left, or Both, with a switch for an R / L switch in the report.

Then add its Zones: a name and a range. Leave From or To empty for a zone without an end. The zones take their colours in order, from green to red, or a colour you choose yourself.

How a block shows it

Under Show as you turn a block into a Graph over time, a Time per zone or a Score card, keeping the variable and its zones. Each has its own options:

BlockOptions
Score cardValue: Its value on the meter, % of time in a zone or Time in a zone, for a zone you pick. With its value: a legend of the zones, with their names and values
Graph over timeA legend of the zones, with their names and values
Time per zoneShow zones as bars or a pie, with the names and values of the zones
Input answerThe field it shows, and zones that colour the answer by its value or its points

Blocks show sample data while you build, so you see the result before any recording is processed.

One block, one question

A block reads best when it shows one variable with zones that mean something, such as Good, Medium and Bad. Put the zone limits and their source in the info text of the block, so the reader of the report knows where they come from.

Findings

The last tab is Findings, where the evaluator writes conclusions for each project. Here you only name its sections, such as Current Situation and Recommended Actions, up to six. Switch it off if the report needs no findings.

6 Save & Publish

Give the template its tile:

  • Name — the title of the tile and the report.
  • Subtext — a short line under the name, such as Upper body posture.
  • Category — the group on the New Project page; create a new one here if needed.
  • Description (optional) — the info text of the tile, with simple formatting. It is also shown on the cover of the PDF report.
  • Image (optional) — an SVG or PNG with a transparent background; it takes the colour of the tile.

Then click Publish. The template is now available under New Project. Save and close keeps it as a draft instead.

7 Changing a Template

Edit a published template the same way. Your changes are a draft until you publish again; the version number then goes up by one.

Saved reports never change

Every report keeps its own copy of the template version it was made with. Editing or deleting a template afterwards does not change reports that were already made.
